The Sicilian: Dragon

1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 g6. Black puts the bishop on g7, where it rakes the long diagonal towards White's queenside. Against the Yugoslav Attack both sides castle on opposite wings and attack at full speed, which is why club players love it.

  • ECO B70–B79
  • For Black
  • Club level
  • 6 White systems covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • The bishop on g7 is your best piece. Never swap it lightly, and be wary of Bh6.
  • The half-open c-file belongs to you: a rook on c8, a knight coming to c4, and the exchange sacrifice on c3.
  • The freeing break is ...d5. If you can play it safely, you have usually equalised.

Typical plans

  • Against the Yugoslav: ...Bd7, ...Rc8, ...Ne5–c4, and meet h4 with ...h5.
  • ...Rxc3, giving up rook for knight to wreck the pawns in front of White's king, is often correct.
  • Against quiet lines: ...Nc6, ...Be6, and ...d5 or queenside play with ...a6 and ...b5.

Pawn structures

  • Dragon structure (d6, e7, g6): solid and flexible, with no weaknesses until you move the e-pawn.
  • After ...d5 and exchanges: an open centre where your bishops are strong.
  • Opposite-side castling: pawn storms on both wings. The side that opens a file first usually wins.

Why the Dragon?

Your pieces go to the same squares almost every game, and the attacking ideas are easy to remember. The main lines are sharp, so the trainer below matters: know the first dozen moves cold.

If they play something odd

Against other sixth moves, play ...Bg7, castle, and develop with ...Nc6. The plan of ...d5, or ...a6 and ...b5, works against almost anything.
