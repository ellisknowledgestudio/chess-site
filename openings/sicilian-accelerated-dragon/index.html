The Sicilian: Accelerated Dragon

1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6. Black fianchettoes at once and keeps the d-pawn at home, hoping to play ...d5 in one move instead of two. That takes the sting out of the Yugoslav Attack. The price is that White can set up the Maróczy Bind with c4.

  • ECO B34–B39
  • For Black
  • Club level
  • 6 White systems covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • By not playing ...d6 you save a move for ...d5. If it comes in one go, you are usually fine.
  • White's Yugoslav set-up with f3 and Qd2 is too slow here: see the 7.f3 line.
  • The critical test is 5.c4, the Maróczy Bind, which stops ...d5 for good.

Typical plans

  • Against the Maróczy: exchange a pair of knights, put the bishop on c6, then ...a5, ...Nd7–c5 and ...Qb6.
  • Against Bc4 set-ups: ...a5 and ...d5, opening the centre at once.
  • In quiet lines: ...d5 as soon as it is safe, with full equality.

Pawn structures

  • Maróczy Bind (c4 and e4 against d6): you have less space but no weaknesses. Play on the dark squares.
  • Open centre (after ...d5): your g7 bishop dominates the long diagonal.
  • Doubled c-pawns (after Nxc6 bxc6): they support ...d5 and open the b-file.

Why the Accelerated Dragon?

You get the Dragon bishop without having to memorise the Yugoslav Attack. The plans are positional and easy to remember, and one trap aside, the opening is very safe.

If they play something odd

Whatever White does, play ...Bg7, ...Nf6 and castle. Then ask whether ...d5 works; if it does, play it.
