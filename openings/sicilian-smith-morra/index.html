The Sicilian: Smith-Morra Gambit

1.e4 c5 2.d4 cxd4 3.c3 — the Smith-Morra Gambit. You give up a pawn to get every piece out quickly: knights on c3 and f3, bishop on c4, queen on e2 and rooks on the open c- and d-files. At club level the practical chances are excellent, and Black needs to know what to do.

  • ECO B21
  • For White
  • Club level
  • 7 Black replies covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • After 3...dxc3 4.Nxc3 you are a pawn down with a clear lead in development and two open files.
  • The standard set-up: Nf3, Bc4, castle, Qe2, and rooks to d1 and c1.
  • The pawn break is e5, opening lines before Black has castled.

Typical plans

  • Pressure on d6 and down the d-file with Rd1 and Bf4.
  • Nb5, hitting c7 and d6, especially when Black's queen is on c7.
  • Sacrifices on e6, f7 and d5 once Black's king is stuck in the middle. Calculate them; do not just hope.

Pawn structures

  • Accepted: no c-pawn or d-pawn for you, open files for your rooks, and a target on d6.
  • Declined with ...d3: a Maróczy Bind with c4, a small safe edge and no gambit.
  • Declined with ...Nf6 or ...d5: you are in the Alapin, with level material.

Why the Smith-Morra?

It takes the Sicilian player out of their preparation on move two and makes them defend. It is risky against a well-prepared opponent, since you are a pawn down if the attack fades, but it is one of the best ways to learn how to use a lead in development.

If they decline

After 3...Nf6 or 3...d5, play the Alapin lines: the positions are the same as in the Alapin guide, so that work is not wasted. After 3...d3 take with the bishop and play c4.
