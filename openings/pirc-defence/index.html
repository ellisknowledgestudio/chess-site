The Pirc Defence

1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6. Black lets White set up a full centre and develops quietly behind it, with the bishop on g7 waiting for the long diagonal to open. It is the King's Indian's cousin against 1.e4: flexible, a little provocative and full of counter-attacking chances.

  • ECO B07–B09
  • For Black
  • Club level
  • 6 White systems covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • 2...Nf6 makes White defend e4, usually with 3.Nc3, which blocks the c-pawn. White's centre is big but has no c4 behind it.
  • Develop with ...g6, ...Bg7 and castle, then hit the centre with ...c5 or ...e5 depending on White's set-up.
  • When White plays Be3 and Qd2, delay castling and start queenside play with ...c6 and ...b5 first.

Typical plans

  • Against the Austrian (f4): ...c5, undermining d4 before the e5 push arrives.
  • Against the Classical (Nf3, Be2): ...Bg4, ...Nc6 and ...e5, as in the King's Indian.
  • Against Bh6 plans: exchange bishops if you have to, keep the king flexible and counter in the centre.

Pawn structures

  • Benoni-style (after ...c5 and d5): queenside majority, play ...Nc7, ...a6 and ...b5.
  • King's Indian-style (after ...e5 and d5): prepare ...f5 with the knight moving from f6.
  • Open centre (after exchanges on d4 or e5): your g7 bishop comes alive on the long diagonal.

Why the Pirc?

It takes 1.e4 players away from their open-game and Sicilian preparation into positions where understanding counts. It pairs well with the King's Indian, as many plans are shared.

If they play something odd

Against slower set-ups such as an early f3 or Bd3, carry on with ...g6, ...Bg7 and castling. If White plays c4, you have reached a King's Indian.
