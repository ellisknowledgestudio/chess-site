The London System

1.d4 and an early Bf4, followed by e3, Nf3, c3 and Nbd2. The same sturdy set-up works against almost anything Black plays, so you spend your time learning plans rather than memorising theory. This page gives you a club-level system and drills it move by move.

  • ECO D02 · A46 · A48
  • For White
  • Club level
  • ...d5, ...g6 & ...c5/...Qb6

Overview

Key ideas

  • Get the dark-squared bishop out to Bf4 before playing e3, so it is not shut in behind your pawns.
  • Build the triangle d4, e3, c3. It is very hard for Black to break down, and c3 also keeps a knight out of b4.
  • The queen's knight goes to d2, not c3, so it supports e4 or e5 and leaves the c-pawn free.

Typical plans

  • When Black plays ...Bd6 to trade bishops, drop back with Bg3. If Black takes, hxg3 opens the h-file for your rook.
  • Against ...d5 set-ups, the jump Ne5 followed by f4 gives you a kingside attack with the d3 bishop pointing at h7.
  • A quiet h3 gives the bishop a safe square on h2 when Black tries ...Nh5 to hunt it.

Watch out for

  • ...Qb6 hitting b2 once your bishop has left c1. Meet it with Qb3 or make sure b2 is covered.
  • ...c5 and ...c4: once the c-pawn reaches c4 your bishop cannot use d3, so it goes to e2 instead.

Why the London?

It is popular at every level, from club leagues to world champions, because it is low on theory and easy to play well when you are short of preparation time. The middlegames are solid, and you keep the chance to attack on the kingside.
