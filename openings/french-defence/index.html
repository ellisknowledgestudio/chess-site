The French Defence

1.e4 e6 followed by 2...d5. Black builds a rock-solid pawn chain, lets White take some space and then hits back at the centre with ...c5. The positions are closed and strategic, so understanding the plans matters more than memorising moves, which makes it a good fit for club players.

  • ECO C00–C19
  • For Black
  • Club level
  • Steinitz, Advance & Tarrasch

Overview

Key ideas

  • 1...e6 prepares 2...d5, so the d5 pawn is backed up by a pawn and White cannot win it or swap it off for nothing.
  • When White pushes e5, the centre locks. Black attacks the chain at its base with ...c5 and often at its head with ...f6.
  • The light-squared bishop on c8 is shut in behind e6. Look for ways to improve or swap it: ...b6 and ...Ba6, ...Bd7–b5, or ...b5 and ...Bb7.

Typical plans

  • Against the Advance, pile up on d4 with ...Nc6, ...Qb6 and a knight heading for f5 via h6 or e7.
  • In the Steinitz, swap on d4, put the bishop on c5 and expand on the queenside with ...a6 and ...b5.
  • Against the Tarrasch, 3...c5 and 4...Qxd5 give an open position with no weak pawns for Black.

Pawn structures

  • Locked chain (Advance, Steinitz): White's d4/e5 against Black's e6/d5. White has space on the kingside; Black plays on the queenside and against d4.
  • Exchange (3.exd5 exd5): symmetrical, and the c8 bishop is free. Develop actively and you are already equal.

Why the French?

It gives you a sound, fighting answer to 1.e4 in which White's early attacks tend to bounce off. You learn one pawn structure and one main break, ...c5, and they carry you through almost every line.
