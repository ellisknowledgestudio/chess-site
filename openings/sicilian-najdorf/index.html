The Sicilian: Najdorf

1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6. The little pawn move keeps White's pieces out of b5 and prepares ...e5 or ...b5. It is the most respected Sicilian of all, the lifelong choice of Fischer and Kasparov, and it gives Black winning chances in every line.

  • ECO B90–B99
  • For Black
  • Club level
  • 6 White systems covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • 5...a6 stops a knight or bishop landing on b5, so you can play ...e5 without being punished.
  • Against quiet sixth moves you play ...e5 and take a share of the centre. Against 6.Bg5 and 6.Bc4 you play ...e6 instead.
  • The queenside is yours: ...b5, ...Bb7 and pressure down the half-open c-file.

Typical plans

  • With ...e5 played: ...Be7, ...Be6, ...Nbd7, then aim for the freeing break ...d5.
  • When White castles long: push ...b5–b4 to drive the c3 knight away, and open the c-file against the king.
  • The exchange sacrifice ...Rxc3 on c3 is a standard weapon once White's king is on the queenside.

Pawn structures

  • ...e5 structure: you have a backward d-pawn and a hole on d5, but active pieces and control of d4 and f4.
  • ...e6 structure (Scheveningen-style): a compact centre on d6 and e6 that is hard to break down.
  • Opposite-side castling: speed decides. Count tempi before every pawn move.

Why the Najdorf?

It is sound at every level, it fights for a win, and the same pawn structure and plans come up again and again. The price is theory: start with the lines here and add depth as your opponents test you.

If they play something odd

Against other sixth moves such as 6.a4 or 6.g3, the same recipe works: ...e5 if the knight can be pushed around, otherwise ...e6, then develop and expand with ...b5.
