The Sicilian: Alapin (2.c3)

1.e4 c5 2.c3 — the Alapin. Instead of walking into the Open Sicilian and its mountain of theory, you prepare d4 so that after ...cxd4 you can take back with the c-pawn and keep two pawns in the centre. This page gives you one answer to each sound Black reply and drills it move by move.

  • ECO B22
  • For White
  • Club level
  • 8 Black replies covered

Overview

Key ideas

  • 2.c3 supports d4. When Black takes with ...cxd4 you recapture cxd4 and keep a pawn pair on e4 and d4, or on e5 and d4.
  • The price is that your knight cannot use c3 yet, so Black hits e4 straight away with 2...Nf6 or 2...d5. Those are the two main lines.
  • Against 2...Nf6 push 3.e5; against 2...d5 take with 3.exd5. Against everything else, play 3.d4 (or 3.Nf3 first against 2...e5).

Typical plans

  • Develop simply: Nf3, a bishop to d3, c4 or b5, castle short, then a knight to c3 once the c-pawn has been exchanged.
  • With the queen on d1 and the pawn on c3, Qa4+ can fork the king and a loose piece on e4, b4 or g4. See the trap below.
  • With an isolated d-pawn, keep the pieces on and aim at the kingside: Bd3, Qe2 or Qd3, rooks to d1 and e1, and a knight heading for e5.

Pawn structures

  • Isolated d-pawn (after 2...d5 or 2...Nc6): you have open lines and active pieces; Black wants exchanges and a blockade on d5.
  • Pawn on e5 (after 2...Nf6 or 2...g6): it cramps Black's kingside. Support it with d4 and Nf3, and be ready for ...d6 or ...f6.
  • Full centre (after 2...d6 or 2...b6): pawns on e4 and d4 with Bd3 behind them. Keep it protected and build up slowly.

Why the Alapin?

You choose the kind of game, not Black. A Najdorf or Dragon player has spent years on the Open Sicilian and often very little on 2.c3. The positions are open and logical, the plans repeat from line to line, and there is far less to memorise.

If they play something odd

Rare tries such as 2...Qa5, 2...a6 or 2...Qc7 do nothing to stop your plan. Play Nf3 and d4, recapture on d4 with the c-pawn, and develop as in the lines below.
