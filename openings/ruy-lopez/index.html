The Ruy Lopez

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 — also called the Spanish Game. The bishop leans on the knight that guards e5, and White builds long-term pressure rather than chasing a quick knockout. This page gives you a club-level system and drills it move by move.

  • ECO C60–C99
  • For White
  • Club level
  • Closed, Berlin & Open

Overview

Key ideas

  • 3.Bb5 does not win e5 at once (after Bxc6 dxc6 and Nxe5, ...Qd4 regains the pawn), but it keeps Black's centre under strain all game.
  • After 3...a6 retreat with Ba4 and, when kicked again, Bb3. The bishop belongs on the a2–g8 diagonal, aiming at f7.
  • Castle, put a rook on e1 to guard e4, then build the ideal centre with c3 and d4.

Typical plans

  • h3 before d4 stops ...Bg4 pinning the f3 knight, which would undermine your centre.
  • The knight tour Nbd2–f1–g3 brings a piece to the kingside where your attack usually comes.
  • When Black expands with ...b5, the lever a4 opens the a-file for your rook.

Pawn structures

  • Closed centre (after d4 and a later d5): White plays on the kingside, Black on the queenside.
  • Tension (d4 v ...e5 kept): the commonest middlegame. Keep the pawns as they are until the trade helps you.

Why the Ruy Lopez?

It has been a top-level favourite for over a century because the pressure lasts. You learn plans that carry into the middlegame, and many club players with Black know far less theory here than they do against the Italian.
