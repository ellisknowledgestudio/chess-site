Opening guides

    Which opening is this?

    Play moves for both sides (click a piece, then its square; arrow keys move around the board and Enter selects). The name updates as you go, and transpositions are recognised. Or click a named book move to follow a line.

    Named moves from here

      Opening names and ECO codes come from the open lichess.org chess-openings list (public domain), shown in British spelling. A few lines are better known by other names: the Lasker-Pelikan (5...e5) is usually called the Sveshnikov, and the Nyezhmetdinov-Rossolimo is usually just the Rossolimo.

      Building a club repertoire

      One answer each

      You need one plan with White, one reply to 1.e4 and one reply to 1.d4. Add answers to 1.c4 and 1.Nf3 later; at club level they often transpose to something you already know.

      Ideas before moves

      Learn where your pieces belong and which pawn break you're aiming for. When an opponent leaves your memorised line, the plan still tells you what to do.

      Drill, then play

      Drill the main line until it's automatic, then test it in quick games online. After each game, check where you left the book and add that branch to your notes.

      Know the traps

      Every opening has a handful of tricks that win games at club level. Know the ones you can spring and the ones you must not fall into.

      Keep going

      Drill your lines with spaced repetition, check a position with Stockfish, or book a lesson if you'd like a repertoire built around your own games.
